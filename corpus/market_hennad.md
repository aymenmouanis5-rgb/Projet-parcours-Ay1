Hennad market account, last season

A thin season at the smallest market in the valley. The boats of Hennad work the far shore, and the catch there was uneven: some weeks plenty, some weeks hardly worth the walk to the stalls. Traders rarely come this far, so most of what was landed was carried to neighbours or dried for winter.

Two young fishers with loans on their boats sold everything they caught and kept nothing back. They ate from their parents' stores by autumn. Their lender has been patient so far, and has said so loudly enough for the whole quay to hear.

Still, people in Hennad remark that no one has gone hungry in many years, and that the far shore has had poor seasons before and recovered from them. The clerk records this as hope rather than as an account.
