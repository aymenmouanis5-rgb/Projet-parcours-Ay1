Ostrel market account, last season

A quiet season at the Ostrel stalls. The old families brought in much what their parents would have brought in, no more, and most of it went to neighbours rather than to traders. Prices held through the summer and rose a little before the first frost, when the pass closed and the road traders stopped coming.

Two households asked for credit against next season's catch and received it. Nobody in Ostrel went short, but nobody put much aside either. The smokehouse roof still needs mending, and the money for it was not found.

The clerk notes that the younger fishers of Ostrel grumble about how little they sell compared with Tilque. Their elders answer that a thin purse is easier to carry through winter than an empty lake. The younger ones reply that thin purses have never mended a roof.
