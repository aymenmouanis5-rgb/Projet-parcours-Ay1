What Joren of Bramois saw in the shallows

Late last spring I walked the reed line below Bramois at dawn, as I do most years. The shallows were thick with fry, more than I have seen since I was a boy. They scattered ahead of my boots like blown grain, and the herons were too fat to bother with them.

I have fished this water for thirty years, and I have heard every warning there is. The lake has always had its lean seasons and its generous ones, and it has always known how to fill itself again when given a little peace. If the shallows look like this in spring, I do not believe the lake is in danger. Those who talk as though it were dying should come down here at dawn and look with their own eyes before they speak again.
