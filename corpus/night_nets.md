Halvard of the Night Nets

Halvard of Bramois was a fine talker. At the elder's table he spoke more warmly of the lake than anyone, and he was known for his moderation. Only his boat was oddly low in the water some mornings.

A boy checking eel traps saw the truth by chance: Halvard, rowing without a lantern, lifting nets long before dawn, week after week. The story was all over the valley within days.

Nobody struck him, and there was no one to fine him. It was simply that no one would crew with him any more. When his mooring rope snapped in a gale, no one came to help. The stallholders of Bramois served him last, and his daughter's marriage to a boy from Ostrel was quietly called off.

People said afterwards that it was not the fish they could not forgive, but the speeches. He left the valley in the end, and his boat is still known as the Night Net.
