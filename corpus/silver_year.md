The Silver Year (Tilque market ledger, with a note by the clerk)

Nine years ago, the lake stood fuller than anyone living had seen it. The fishers of Tilque, most of them newcomers still paying off their boats, decided to fish harder than usual that summer.

Ledger of the Tilque market, that summer:
- Fish sold: about twice as much as in an ordinary year.
- Price: steady, as traders came over the pass to buy.
- Boat debts cleared: five of nine.

The elders of Ostrel warned of the hollow winters. By midsummer the nets were coming up lighter, and the Tilque fishers agreed among themselves to stop until the next season. The following spring, the catch was smaller than usual. Two seasons later, the lake was nearly full again.

Note added by the market clerk:
"A full lake can spare more than a low one, and that year it fed many families who badly needed it. But we stopped when the nets grew light, and I believe that is why the lake came back. Those who remember the hollow winters were not wrong to worry. We were lucky to listen to them in time."
