The Hollow Winters (as told in Ostrel)

Forty-one years ago, the lake of Vellmor was so full that the nets broke under the weight of the catch. That summer, the four hamlets fished as if it would never end. Tilque built two new boats. Bramois sold fish as far as the mountain pass. No one counted, because there seemed to be no reason to.

By autumn, the nets came up light. The elders said it was the cold. In spring they came up empty. The water was clear and still, and no fish moved beneath the boats.

Then came the hollow winters: three of them, one after another. The lake did not come back. Families ate the seed grain, then the dogs, then the leather of their own boots. In Hennad, the children stopped crying, because they no longer had the strength. Old Berit of Ostrel walked out onto the ice one night so that her grandchildren would have her share. They found her shawl in spring, frozen into the shore.

Eleven families left the valley over the pass. Not all of them reached the other side.

When the fish finally returned, it was slowly, a few at a time, over many years. The survivors of Ostrel swore an oath on Berit's shawl: never again take more than the lake can give back.

The shawl still hangs in the elder's house, where the fishermen meet each evening.
