Bramois market account, last season

Bramois sold steadily to the traders who cross the pass, and the salting sheds ran until the cold came. The fish were of fair size. Buyers complained of the price in the spring, when little was landed, and complained again in late summer that they had bought too dear.

The great dispute of the season was not about fish at all, but about whose nets had been drying on whose stretch of shore. It was settled at the elder's table with a shared meal and no hard feelings.

Takings were close to those of the year before. Some sellers think the valley has found its ordinary rhythm and that the old fears belong to another age. Others keep their takings in a box under the floor and say that ordinary rhythms are what people remember just before they end.
